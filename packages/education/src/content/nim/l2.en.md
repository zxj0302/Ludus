# Nim — XOR's Magic for Combinatorial Games

## 1. Play

Nim is one of the simplest games in the world: a few piles of stones, two players take turns removing any number of stones (at least 1, possibly the whole pile) from a single pile. The player who takes the last stone wins.

Give it a try! Can you find the secret to beating the AI?

## 2. Think

- Why is [1, 2, 3] a losing position, no matter what move you make?
- Why does [3, 4, 5] give you a winning chance?
- Is there a "magic formula" that tells you who wins at a glance?

## 3. Reveal

In 1901, Harvard mathematician Charles Bouton discovered the secret to winning at Nim — the **Nim-sum**:

$$\text{Nim-sum} = a_1 \oplus a_2 \oplus \cdots \oplus a_k$$

where $\oplus$ is the XOR (exclusive OR) operation.

- **Nim-sum = 0 → P-position (Previous player wins)**: the player who just moved is winning; when it's your turn, it's already too late.
- **Nim-sum ≠ 0 → N-position (Next player wins)**: you are guaranteed to win! Just find a pile where removing stones leaves `current pile XOR nim-sum` stones.

**Intuition**: XOR = 0 is like a "perfect balance" — however you disturb it, your opponent can always restore it in one move.

## 4. Solve

For the position [3, 4, 5]:

```
3 = 011 (binary)
4 = 100
5 = 101
XOR = 010 = 2 ≠ 0 → N-position, you win!

Winning move: look at pile 3 (011)
011 ⊕ 010 = 001 = 1 < 3
Take 2 stones from pile 3 → position becomes [1, 4, 5]
Verify: 1 ⊕ 4 ⊕ 5 = 000 = 0 → opponent is now in a P-position!
```

## 5. Create

Nim level generation is elegantly simple: randomly generate a P-position (Nim-sum = 0), then take one random step away to produce an N-position as the puzzle. Difficulty is controlled by the number of piles and the maximum stones per pile — more piles and larger numbers make XOR mental arithmetic harder.
