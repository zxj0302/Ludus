## 1. Play · Play

Two piles of stones, up to 10 each. On your turn, you can:
- **Remove from one pile**: take any number from a single pile
- **Remove from both**: take the **same number** from both piles

The player who takes the last stone wins.

You may choose to go first or let the AI go first.

## 2. Think · Think

Wythoff's game was published by Dutch mathematician Willem Abraham Wythoff in 1907.

Like Nim, it looks simple — but the "remove from both" move changes everything. The winning strategy no longer relies on XOR, but on a surprising constant from nature: the golden ratio.

Can you spot the pattern? Play a few games and see which (a, b) positions the first player always loses from.

## 3. Reveal · Reveal

The **P-positions** (losing for the first player) in Wythoff's game are given by **Beatty sequences**:

$$( \lfloor k\varphi \rfloor, \lfloor k\varphi^2 \rfloor ), \quad k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$$

where $\varphi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.618$ is the **golden ratio**, and $\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1 \approx 2.618$.

The first few P-positions: $(0,0), (1,2), (3,5), (4,7), (6,10), (8,13), \ldots$

**Key property**: If $(a, b)$ is a P-position with $a \le b$, then $b - a = k$ and $a = \lfloor k \cdot \varphi \rfloor$.

For any position $(a, b)$ with $a \le b$, let $k = b - a$:
- If $a = \lfloor k \cdot \varphi \rfloor$ → **P-position** (first player loses with perfect play)
- Otherwise → **N-position** (first player wins)

This is as elegant as Nim's XOR rule, but uses the golden ratio.

Complexity: O(maxPile) — check all legal moves to find one that reaches a P-position.

## 4. Solve · Solve

How the solver works:

1. Compute $k = |b - a|$
2. Check $a = \lfloor k \cdot \varphi \rfloor$ (assuming $a \le b$)
3. If N-position, enumerate all legal moves to find one reaching a P-position
4. The AI always tries to make a winning move; if none exists (P-position), it removes 1 from the larger pile

## 5. Create · Create

The level generator creates random piles (1–10 stones each). P and N positions occur naturally. The player chooses whether to go first to claim the advantageous position.
