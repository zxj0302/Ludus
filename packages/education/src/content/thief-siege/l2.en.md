# Thief Siege — Block the Escape Route

## 1. 玩 · Play

You play the cops. Each turn, click an open floor cell to place one cop. The thief then moves one step toward an edge exit. If the thief reaches an exit, it escapes; if every route is blocked, your siege succeeds.

v1.1 uses larger boards: easy is 11×11, medium is 15×15, and hard is 19×19. Try playing first: watch not only where the thief wants to go now, but also which backup route it may switch to next.

## 2. 思考 · Think

- Why does the thief keep drifting in one direction?
- Is it always best to block beside the thief, or better to block a bottleneck?
- On an irregular board, which cells act like natural choke points?
- If you block the current shortest route, what alternative route can the thief switch to?

## 3. 揭示 · Reveal

Model the board as a graph: every walkable cell is a vertex, neighboring cells are edges, and edge cells are exits. The thief's immediate objective is to find a shortest path from its current cell to any exit.

Placing a cop removes one vertex from the graph. A good placement lengthens the shortest path; a great placement blocks a bottleneck and may disconnect every escape route.

v1.1 also accounts for the thief fighting back: the AI uses adversarial search to estimate how future cop placements may block future escape routes, instead of only following the current shortest path. The education visualization still shows the BFS escape path because it is the clearest way to see what your placement is cutting.

## 4. 求解 · Solve

The solver first runs BFS on the current position:

```text
enqueue the thief cell
expand walkable neighbors layer by layer
first reached exit => shortest escape path
queue exhausted without an exit => thief is trapped
```

The highlighted cells in the visualization are the path reconstructed by BFS. When you press "Solve", the recommended cop move comes from the same adversarial engine: small boards try to prove the result, larger boards search within a budget, and timeout cases fall back to greedy BFS.

## 5. 出题 · Create

The generator creates an 11×11, 15×15, or 19×19 irregular mask, marks its boundary as exits, adds a few interior obstacles, and starts the thief near the center.

Easy levels are checked with adversarial search: with proper play, the cops can win. Medium and hard levels are larger, so the generator uses bottleneck construction plus shallow adversarial checks. That is an honest engineering guarantee, not a mathematical proof of the full game tree.

Difficulty mainly comes from board size, cop budget, and openness: larger boards, fewer cops, and fewer obstacles give the thief more alternatives.
