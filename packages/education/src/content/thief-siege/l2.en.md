# Thief Siege — Block the Escape Route

## 1. 玩 · Play

You play the cops. Each turn, click an open floor cell to place one cop. The thief then moves one step along a current shortest route toward an edge exit. If the thief reaches an exit, it escapes; if every route is blocked, your siege succeeds.

Try playing first: watch where the thief wants to go, then make that route narrower each turn.

## 2. 思考 · Think

- Why does the thief keep drifting in one direction?
- Is it always best to block beside the thief, or better to block a bottleneck?
- On an irregular board, which cells act like natural choke points?

## 3. 揭示 · Reveal

Model the board as a graph: every walkable cell is a vertex, neighboring cells are edges, and edge cells are exits. The thief's immediate objective is to find a shortest path from its current cell to any exit.

Placing a cop removes one vertex from the graph. A good placement lengthens the shortest path; a great placement blocks a bottleneck and may disconnect every escape route.

This implementation does not claim a full minimax solution to the pursuit-evasion game. It focuses on the core graph idea: **BFS finds an escape path, and cops try to cut that path**.

## 4. 求解 · Solve

The solver runs BFS on the current position:

```
enqueue the thief cell
expand walkable neighbors layer by layer
first reached exit => shortest escape path
queue exhausted without an exit => thief is trapped
```

The highlighted cells in the visualization are the path reconstructed by BFS. When you press "Solve", the recommended cop move prioritizes cells on that path.

## 5. 出题 · Create

The generator creates a 7×7, 9×9, or 11×11 irregular mask, marks its boundary as exits, adds a few interior obstacles, and starts the thief near the center. It then verifies with BFS that at least one escape path exists.

Difficulty mainly comes from board size, cop budget, and openness: larger boards, fewer cops, and fewer obstacles give the thief more alternatives.
