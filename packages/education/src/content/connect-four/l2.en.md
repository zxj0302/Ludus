# Connect Four — Every Drop Is a Decision

## 1. Play

Drop discs into seven columns; first to connect four in a row (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal) wins. The center column is often strongest—can you beat the AI?

## 2. Think

- If the opponent threatens four next turn, which column must you block?
- Can a move look safe but actually hand them the win?
- How does the search tree explode as the board fills?

## 3. Reveal

Connect Four is a **perfect-information, zero-sum, alternating** two-player game. Classical solving uses **Minimax**: both players assumed optimal, future positions enumerated.

**Alpha-Beta pruning** cuts branches that cannot improve the bound—far fewer nodes than naive Minimax. The live analysis trace samples that search.

The standard 7×6 board was **strongly solved by Allis (1988)** (**first player wins**; perfect play ends on move 41 per [Christophe Steininger c4](https://github.com/ChristopheSteininger/c4)). This Hub build uses **depth-limited** Alpha-Beta—honest AI, not a full opening book.

### 7×6 perfect play (strong solution)

On the standard 7×6 board, optimal play from both sides is **strongly solved** (consistent with [Christophe Steininger c4](https://github.com/ChristopheSteininger/c4) and Allis 1988):

| Item | Result |
|------|--------|
| Perfect-play outcome | **First player wins** |
| Perfect game length | Ends on move **41** |

**Choosing sides:**

- **I go first**: with perfect play the first player wins—you could theoretically force a win; this Hub AI uses depth-limited Alpha-Beta, **not** a strong solver, so real games may differ.
- **AI goes first**: with perfect play the first player (AI) wins—the second player can only delay defeat.

Key work: Allis (1988), Allen (1988), John Tromp, [Pascal Pons connect4](https://github.com/PascalPons/connect4), [Steininger c4](https://github.com/ChristopheSteininger/c4).
